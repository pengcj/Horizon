## [Silicon quantum device achieves 99% fidelity two-qubit gates and teleportation.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10423-9) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A silicon-based device using mobile spin qubits demonstrated a two-qubit gate fidelity of about 99% and performed quantum state teleportation between qubits separated by 320 nanometers. This breakthrough demonstrates a scalable architecture for quantum processors by using mobile qubits that can be shuttled within a circuit, a key step toward building practical, large-scale quantum computers on a proven semiconductor platform. The two-qubit gate was achieved by shuttling two electron spins together by a total displacement of 240 nm, and the teleportation was conditional and post-selected, with an average gate fidelity of 87%.

rss · Nature · May 6, 00:00

**Background**: Spin qubits in silicon are a leading platform for quantum computing because they can be fabricated using existing semiconductor technology. Quantum teleportation is a fundamental protocol for transferring quantum information between distant locations without physically moving the qubit itself. High-fidelity two-qubit gates are essential for performing complex quantum algorithms and error correction.

## [Purcell-Enhanced Spin-Phonon Coupling Achieved with Diamond Color Center](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10495-7) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers experimentally observed the acoustic Purcell effect by engineering a microwave-frequency nanomechanical resonator around a single diamond color center spin qubit, thereby significantly enhancing the spin-phonon coupling strength. This breakthrough is a key step toward building hybrid quantum systems and efficient quantum transducers, as it demonstrates a method to strongly couple a solid-state spin qubit to mechanical motion, which could enable new interfaces between different quantum platforms. The experiment specifically utilized a nitrogen-vacancy (NV) color center in diamond, and the engineered resonator operated at microwave frequencies to match the spin transition energy, which is essential for observing the Purcell enhancement in the acoustic domain.

rss · Nature · May 6, 00:00

**Background**: The Purcell effect describes how the spontaneous emission rate of a quantum emitter can be enhanced by placing it in a resonant cavity. In this context, the 'acoustic' Purcell effect refers to enhancing the emission of phonons (quantized sound vibrations) rather than photons. Diamond color centers, such as the NV center, are point defects in the diamond lattice that host electron spins which can be used as qubits. Spin-phonon coupling refers to the interaction between these spin qubits and mechanical vibrations, which is a fundamental mechanism for controlling spins and transferring quantum information between different physical systems.

Researchers used a quantum twisting microscope to directly image the interacting energy bands of magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene, revealing the dual nature of its electrons as both heavy and light particles depending on momentum. This breakthrough provides the first direct experimental visualization of how electron interactions reshape the fundamental band structure in a key quantum material, offering crucial insights into the mechanisms behind its exotic correlated phenomena like superconductivity. The technique, quantum twisting microscopy, enables momentum-space imaging of electrons, analogous to how a scanning tunneling microscope probes real space, and was applied to the system at its 'magic angle' of approximately 1.1 degrees.

rss · Nature · May 6, 00:00

**Background**: Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene consists of two graphene layers stacked with a slight twist, which at a specific 'magic angle' creates flat electronic bands where electrons move very slowly, leading to strong correlations and emergent phenomena like superconductivity. Flat bands are a key concept in condensed matter physics where the kinetic energy of electrons is quenched, making electron-electron interactions dominant and enabling novel quantum phases. The quantum twisting microscope is a recently developed scanning probe tool that can perform local interference experiments to directly probe quantum mechanical properties in momentum space.

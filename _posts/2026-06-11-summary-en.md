The specific binding is mediated by VDAC1, a major mitochondrial outer membrane channel for metabolite transport, and RANBP2, a key component of the nuclear pore complex. This interaction ensures a direct and efficient ATP supply to meet the high energy demands of nuclear processes.

rss · Nature · Jun 10, 00:00

**Background**: Mitochondria are the primary energy-producing organelles in cells, generating ATP through oxidative phosphorylation. The nuclear pore complex (NPC) is a massive protein assembly embedded in the nuclear envelope that regulates the transport of molecules between the cytoplasm and the nucleus. Previously, it was thought that ATP diffused freely from mitochondria through the cytosol to reach the nucleus.

## [Two-Component Exciton Condensates Achieved in Electron-Hole Bilayer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10636-y) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have experimentally realized macroscopic quantum coherence in two-component exciton Bose-Einstein condensates within MoSe₂/hBN/WSe₂ electron-hole bilayers, observing distinct spin-valley polarized phases and quantum phase transitions induced by magnetic fields up to approximately 1.8 K. This breakthrough provides a new platform to study macroscopic quantum phenomena and quantum phase transitions in strongly interacting bosonic systems, advancing fundamental understanding of Bose-Einstein condensation and potentially informing the development of future quantum technologies based on van der Waals heterostructures. The condensate forms in a heterostructure where a thin hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) layer separates electron-rich MoSe₂ and hole-rich WSe₂ monolayers, enabling the formation of interlayer excitons with long lifetimes. The observation of distinct spin-valley polarized phases under a magnetic field highlights the interplay between the valley degree of freedom and magnetic interactions in these 2D materials.

rss · Nature · Jun 10, 00:00

**Background**: A Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) is a state of matter occurring at very low temperatures where particles, or quasiparticles like excitons, collapse into the same quantum ground state, exhibiting macroscopic quantum coherence. Excitons are bound pairs of an electron and a hole, and in van der Waals heterostructures (artificial stacks of 2D materials like MoSe₂ and WSe₂ separated by an insulator), they can form 'interlayer' excitons with extended lifetimes. Spin-valley coupling in transition metal dichalcogenides links the electron's spin state to its valley index in momentum space, creating a rich platform for studying spintronics and valleytronics.

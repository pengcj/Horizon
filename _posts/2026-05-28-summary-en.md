## [4 ppm Precision Measurement of Antihydrogen Hyperfine Splitting](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10556-x) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The ALPHA experiment at CERN has measured the ground-state hyperfine splitting of antihydrogen with an unprecedented precision of 4 parts per million (ppm). This level of accuracy makes the measurement sensitive to the internal structure of the antiproton itself. This result represents a major breakthrough in antimatter physics, enabling new tests of CPT symmetry, a fundamental principle stating that the laws of physics are the same for matter and antimatter. If any deviation were found in this splitting compared to normal hydrogen, it would signal a profound violation of this symmetry and challenge the Standard Model. The hyperfine splitting corresponds to the energy difference between states where the antiproton and positron spins are parallel and antiparallel, analogous to the famous 21 cm line in hydrogen. Reaching 4 ppm precision is a critical threshold where corrections due to the antiproton's finite size and internal structure become relevant, moving beyond simple point-particle models.

rss · Nature · May 27, 00:00

**Background**: Antihydrogen is the antimatter counterpart of hydrogen, consisting of an antiproton and a positron. The hyperfine splitting is a small energy shift in atomic energy levels caused by the interaction between the magnetic moments of the nucleus and the orbiting electron (or positron). The ALPHA experiment at CERN traps antihydrogen atoms using magnetic fields to perform precision spectroscopy, comparing them to hydrogen to test fundamental symmetries like CPT (Charge, Parity, Time) invariance.

Somatic mosaicism refers to the presence of genetically different cell populations within a single individual arising from mutations after fertilization.

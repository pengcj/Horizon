## [Proposal to Improve BPF Loop Verification Using Scalar Evolution](https://lwn.net/Articles/1076121/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Eduard Zingerman presented a proposal to enhance the BPF verifier's loop analysis by incorporating scalar evolution (SCEV) techniques to better handle nested loops and avoid instruction limit errors. This improvement addresses a long-standing limitation in the BPF verifier's static analysis, which can cause it to erroneously hit instruction limits on loops that should be analyzable, thereby impacting performance and correctness of BPF programs. The work is in-progress and was presented at the Linux Storage, Filesystem, Memory-Management, and BPF Summit. Scalar evolution is a compiler analysis technique used to understand how scalar variables change across loop iterations, which could allow the verifier to reason about loops more efficiently.

rss · LWN.net · Jun 9, 13:37

**Background**: The BPF verifier is a critical kernel component that statically checks BPF programs for safety before execution. Currently, it analyzes loops by simulating each iteration, which can lead to hitting a limit on the number of analyzed instructions, especially for nested loops. Scalar evolution is a well-established compiler optimization technique (used in GCC and LLVM) that analyzes the mathematical behavior of variables across loop iterations to enable more efficient reasoning about loop bounds and termination.

## [Scientists crack mystery of initial ice crystal formation in freezing liquids.](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01817-w) ⭐️ 6.0/10

New experimental studies are revealing the earliest moments of ice crystal formation, demonstrating that long-standing classical theories about ice growth are wildly inaccurate when compared with actual data. This progress is significant as it challenges fundamental assumptions in physics and could improve models in critical fields like climate science, materials science, and cryobiology that rely on understanding freezing processes. Research indicates that the classical nucleation theory (CNT) is insufficient, with newer work highlighting non-classical pathways, such as surface-induced crystallization involving precursors and negative pressure, which are now being experimentally observed.

rss · Nature · Jun 9, 00:00

**Background**: Classical nucleation theory (CNT) has been the dominant model for describing how new phases, like ice, form from a metastable state. It models nucleation as a single-step process where a small, stable nucleus of the new phase forms and then grows. However, for ice formation in water, this model has proven difficult to verify and increasingly appears incomplete, as it does not account for complex, multi-step pathways observed in experiments.

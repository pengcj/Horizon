Researchers have programmed the RNA-guided nuclease CRISPR-Cas12a2 to target cancer-specific transcripts, activating a process that shreds chromatin and selectively induces lethal DNA damage in cancer cells. This novel therapeutic approach provides a precision treatment strategy for cancers driven by previously 'undruggable' targets, representing a significant paradigm shift in targeted cancer therapy. The method exploits the trans-nucleolytic cleavage activity of CRISPR-Cas12a2; upon binding its RNA target, the nuclease undergoes a conformational change that triggers non-specific degradation of the entire chromatin structure within the cancer cell.

**Background**: CRISPR-Cas12a2 is an RNA-guided nuclease, a type of molecular scissors that can be programmed to target specific genetic sequences. Chromatin is the complex of DNA and proteins that packages genetic material within the cell nucleus. This research repurposes the enzyme's inherent 'catalytic promiscuity'—initially seen as a drawback for precise editing—into a therapeutic weapon.

Researchers have published the first integrated connectome of a fruit fly's entire brain and spinal cord (ventral nerve cord), revealing that neural control circuits are widely distributed rather than organized in a strict hierarchy. This discovery challenges the long-held hierarchical model of neural control and provides a new paradigm for understanding how the brain and body coordinate movement and behavior, which is fundamental to systems neuroscience and control theory. The connectome maps every neuron and synapse between the brain and the ventral nerve cord in an adult Drosophila melanogaster, bridging a critical gap that previously prevented understanding of how information flows from the brain to the body.

rss · Nature · Jun 8, 00:00

**Background**: A connectome is a comprehensive map of neural connections within a nervous system, often described as its wiring diagram. Previous connectomes were limited to either the brain or the spinal cord separately, leaving the crucial interface between them unmapped. The fruit fly (Drosophila melanogaster) is a key model organism in neuroscience due to its relatively small nervous system yet complex behaviors.
